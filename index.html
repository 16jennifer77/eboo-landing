01The protocol

Your blood, filtered and returned.

EBOO stands for Extracorporeal Blood Oxygenation and Ozonation. Two IV lines form a closed loop between you and the machine. Blood leaves through one line, passes through a dialysis-style filtration membrane, and returns through the other. Nothing about the loop is rushed, and it stays closed from start to finish.

Inside the filter, your blood moves alongside fine membrane tubules carrying a medical-grade mixture of oxygen and ozone. The gas disperses across the membrane as blood passes, and what the membrane catches stays in the circuit. Some clinics call the method ozone dialysis. We simply call it EBOO.

02The hour

Five stations, one closed loop.

The whole treatment happens in the chair, in one continuous pass. Watch the loop, then read it station by station.

01

Settle in

You take a recliner, not a procedure table. Your nurse checks vitals, answers questions, and places two IV lines, one for each direction of the loop.

02

Blood draws out

A gentle pump moves blood from the first line into the circuit. Flow is slow and steady, and your nurse watches it the entire time.

03

Through the filter

Blood passes through a semi-permeable dialysis-style membrane. What the membrane catches stays in the circuit rather than going back to you.

04

Oxygen meets blood

Fine tubules inside the membrane carry a medical-grade mix of oxygen and ozone. The gas disperses across the membrane as your blood moves past.

05

Back to you

Filtered, oxygenated blood returns through the second line. When the pass is complete, lines come out, and most clients head straight back to their day.

Every circuit is sterile and single-use. Your medical team reviews your health history before any treatment is scheduled.
